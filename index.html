01

Beacon · LFG for co-op games

Beacon is a looking for group platform for finding people to play games with. I kept hitting the same problem: a game I wanted to play, no queue or find-a-squad function in it, and none of my friends online or interested. Peak and Subnautica are good examples. Both are a lot more fun with other people, but there's no way to just hop in and get matched with someone.

You sign in with Discord and can optionally link Steam. Discord because that's where gamers already are, which also meant I didn't have to host voice chat myself and take on the security problems that come with it. If you link Steam you can import your whole library or just your most played games. The game data comes from Twitch's database, so almost every multiplayer game is in there with its cover art.

An LFG site isn't a new idea, so I focused on the things other ones don't do and on keeping it easy to use. No email signup, no ads, no cluttered UI. Groups can have invite-only links, which I built thinking about Twitch streamers hosting games for their viewers. The Beacon logo pulses whenever a post goes live. That kind of detail is something I notice and appreciate on other sites, so I put it in mine.

Every access rule lives in Postgres row-level security instead of the front end, so "only the host can approve" and "only accepted players see the invite" hold even if someone talks to the API directly. Approvals lock the group row so two people accepted at the same moment can't overfill a lobby. Steam linking goes through OpenID and gets verified server-side so accounts can't be spoofed. The slur filter runs in the database and normalizes leetspeak, spacing and repeated letters before it matches, with the term list stored encoded so it isn't readable from the client.

The site is finished and ready for people to use. The hard part now is advertising, because getting that first group of users is the real hurdle for anything like this. Either way I got a lot out of building it, especially learning Supabase and Vercel.

02

10" 6U Homelab Rack

A 6U ten-inch server rack I designed from scratch in Blender and printed on a Bambu X2D. It's built around two pairs of Gator Rackworks steel rails, so everything mounts on real 10-32 threads instead of printed ones. Every 1U module is separate: base and top frame with slot grille, a dual mount holding a Pi 5 and 4B side by side, switch shelf, PDU, 12-port patch panel, fan rack, blanks. You can rearrange the layout or reprint one piece without touching the rest.

The side panels are two layers, a 3mm inner panel and a 1.6mm outer cover, split into halves so every piece fits a 256mm bed. The cover pieces get mirrored before slicing so the textured PEI face ends up on the outside where you can see it. Slot cutouts in the rear and top panels are spaced to match a 120mm fan's bolt pattern, so extra fans drop straight in without drilling anything. The Pi 5 mount sits high to clear an NVMe HAT underneath and a tower cooler on top.

Two parts are modified from other creators and credited on the model page: the TL-SG108E switch rack and the 4.3" display mount.

03

Self-Hosted Network Stack

The two Pis in that rack handle DNS and remote access for the whole apartment. Pi-hole v6 does the filtering, with Unbound behind it as a local recursive resolver so lookups go to the root servers instead of a third-party upstream. Tailscale gets me in from outside the LAN, which matters because the unit is on GPON fiber and I have no admin access to the gateway. No port forwarding, no DHCP reservations, so everything has to work without them.

A small Flask service exposes live host metrics over the LAN, managed by systemd so it survives reboots. The Pi 4B polls that endpoint and renders the stats on a 4.3" DSI panel mounted in the rack.

04

Spotify Desk Companion

A desk device that shows what's playing, scrolls synced lyrics, and draws a live audio visualizer. A Pi 4B with a touchscreen runs the UI in pygame, with invisible touch zones for transport controls. On the PC side a companion app captures system audio over WASAPI loopback, runs an FFT, and streams the spectrum plus BPM and loudness to the Pi over UDP. Track data comes from the Spotify Web API, polled with rate limits in mind and a faster poll window right after a skip so it keeps up.

06

BMW N55 Diagnostics & Repair

I maintain my E90 BMW (N55 turbo six) myself, diagnosis included. Cooling system, fuel system, oil pan gasket, full transmission service with new seals and mechatronics. Those last two are jobs most people hand to a shop because of how much has to come apart to reach them.

I use ISTA, which is BMW's actual dealer software, along with BimmerLink and Bimmergeeks ProTool. Those get me fault codes and live data from every module in the car instead of the generic codes a cheap OBD reader gives you. It matters because a lot of these problems feel identical from the driver's seat. A misfire could be coils, plugs, injectors or the high pressure fuel pump, and the only way to know is to watch fuel pressure while the engine is running.

I also flash the engine and transmission control units with MHD and xHP. That's firmware going onto embedded modules over the car's diagnostic bus. A bad flash bricks the unit.
